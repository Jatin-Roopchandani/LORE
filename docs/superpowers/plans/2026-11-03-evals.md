# Evaluation plan: does the new default behaviour actually help?

**Why.** PR #1 shipped about ten answer behaviours **on by default**: prose answers, story order, readings, speaker attribution, strict links, role coverage, thematic parts, timeline, interpretive answers and deep answers. Only FULL_ANSWERS and NO_RESTATE have a completed paired eval. Tuning since then has been driven by single questions about one book, which is how overfitting happens. This plan measures each behaviour on **blind sets that were never used for tuning**, using pre-registered bars. Behaviours that don't clear their bar get switched off.

**Budget reality.** Gemini's free tier allows about 1,000 requests a day across two Flash-Lite models. Approximate cost per question:
- an ordinary answer: about 8 requests;
- an interpretive answer: about 12–16;
- a deep answer: about 40–45.

A paired run asks every question twice, once with the behaviour on and once with it off. The plan therefore runs over **five quota days**, and each day is resumable: `run_pair` stops cleanly at the limit and continues the next day. **Grading uses Claude agents, not Gemini.**

## Ground rules

- **Pinned code.** Each run executes from a detached worktree at a fixed commit, as round 6 did with `~/LORE-r6`. Edits made during the run cannot leak into it.
- **One model per question.** `run_pair` alternates the two Gemini models between questions, and alternates which arm goes first.
- **Blind grading.** `grade_pair packet --answer-only` hides which arm wrote which answer and shuffles them. The key is sealed until scoring.
- **Bars are fixed before the run.** They are written below and must not change after results are in.
- **No tuning on these sets.** Every question in arc, interpretive and deep is held out. A fix suggested by these results must be re-checked on a *new* set before it is adopted.
- **Avoid the WSL crash pattern.** Run no other heavy work (full test suites, builds) while an eval runs. Before starting, give WSL more memory if needed (`.wslconfig`).

## Day 0 (no Gemini): grade round 6

Round 6 already exists. Its arms were the prose bundle at 12e9dbc, on versus off, over dev 56 + held-out 16, and every answer is complete.

1. Build answer-only packets for r6 (dev) and h6 (held-out).
2. Grade them blind with Claude agents, following GRADING.md: key facts, false claims, duplicates, and a readability preference.
3. Score them against the pre-registered prose bar in the prose spec, §6:
   - false claims no higher than control;
   - key facts no worse than −4 on dev, with no loss on held-out;
   - readability preferred, with sign-test p < 0.05;
   - fallback rate at or below 25%.

This tells us whether the original prose bundle earned its default.

## Day 1: arc set, timeline and story order (about 350 requests)

- **Arms:** current HEAD with `timeline=on,story_order=on` versus `timeline=off,story_order=off`.
- **Set:** arc (A1–A16), questions about change, relationships and loyalty.
- **Bar** (story-beats spec §7):
  - false claims no higher than control;
  - a key-fact gain on arc, sign test p < 0.05 over non-tied pairs;
  - no more than a 5-point increase in fallback rate.

## Day 2: interpretive set, the interpretive mode (about 450 requests)

- **Arms:** `interpretive=on` versus `interpretive=off`.
- **Set:** interpretive (I1–I16).
- **Bar** (interpretive spec §7):
  - false facts no higher than control;
  - a position taken where warranted in at least 12 of 16 answers;
  - a counter-case present where expected in at least 10 of 16;
  - **zero** inferences stated as book facts;
  - readability preference p < 0.05.

## Days 2–3: deep set, deep answers (about 500 requests)

- **Arms:** `deep=on` (depth deep) versus `deep=off` (focused).
- **Set:** deep (W1–W10).
- **Bar** (deep spec, "Evaluation additions"):
  - false facts no higher than control;
  - average coverage of the listed aspects at least 70%;
  - coverage above control on at least 8 of 10 questions;
  - readability preference p < 0.05.

## Days 3–5: regression check, all current defaults versus all off (about 1,300 requests)

- **Arms:** current HEAD defaults versus every new behaviour switched off. That is the earlier pipeline, plus retrieval v2 and the resolver fixes, which have no switch.
- **Sets:** dev 56 and held-out 16.
- **Bar:**
  - false claims no higher than control on both sets;
  - key facts no worse than −4 on dev, with no loss on held-out.

This guards against the new behaviours hurting ordinary factual questions.

## Deciding

- **A behaviour that clears its bar** keeps its default, recorded in GRADING.md under "Decisions".
- **A behaviour that misses its bar** has its switch set to off in a small PR, with the numbers in the PR description. If the failure suggests a fix, that fix is spec'd separately and evaluated on a *fresh* question set.
- Everything is recorded: run labels, commits, scores, the p-values and the decision.

## What to prepare before Day 1

- Grading packets for the arc, interpretive and deep sets need set-specific rubrics: aspects for deep, and positions and counter-case for interpretive. Check that `grade_pair` supports their fields, or add a small grader template per set.
- `run_pair` arms exist for each switch. Check `story_order`, `timeline`, `interpretive` and `deep` once more.
- A driver script, like `round6.sh`, per day. It waits for the Pacific reset, runs from the pinned worktree, and logs to `~/.cache/lore/`.
